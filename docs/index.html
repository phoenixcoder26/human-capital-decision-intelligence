Statistical Findings
533 eligible employees were selected from the 1,000-employee synthetic workforce dataset using flight risk, retention priority, career stall, and engagement criteria.
266 treatment / 267 control — groups were statistically balanced on all 14 pre-intervention characteristics, confirming valid randomisation.
The enhanced intervention improved engagement by +4.6 points more than control in the simulated data (95% CI 4.10–5.19, p < 0.001, Significant), a large standardised difference (Cohen's d = 1.44).
Flight-risk score decreased more in the treatment group in the simulated data (difference −0.053, 95% CI −0.060 to −0.047, p < 0.001), with the reduction strongest among high-performing employees with high baseline risk scores.
Business Interpretation
What the Results Mean for HR
Observed attrition rate difference: 0.3 pp (14.3% treatment vs 14.6% control), not statistically significant (p = 0.916; adjusted odds ratio 0.91, 95% CI 0.54–1.51 includes 1). No protective effect can be claimed.
High-performing flight risks and career-stalled employees responded most strongly in the simulation — a hypothesis to test in a real pilot, not a conclusion about real employees.
In the simulation, engagement and flight-risk scores moved but attrition did not — whether engagement gains lead to lower attrition would need a longer, real-world pilot.
Next steps (for a real pilot): run a randomised pilot with real employees before any scale-up decision, test segment-priority ordering as a hypothesis, and integrate findings into the causal inference module.
