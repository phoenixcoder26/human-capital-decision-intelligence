How the experiment was set up

A two-group pilot on synthetic employees: who qualified, how they were split, and how each result was judged.

533 of 1,000 synthetic employees qualified. They were split at random into 266 treatment and 267 control, and three outcomes were judged by tests fixed in advance.

1. Find who qualifies

Each dot is one of the 1,000 synthetic employees. An employee qualifies by meeting any one of five risk signals.

The signals overlap, so the five counts add up to more than the 533 employees (53.3%) who qualify.

2. Split them at random

A random draw (fixed seed) puts each qualifying employee in one of two groups. Both groups keep every normal HR support; the treatment group gets an extra pilot on top.

3. Measure three outcomes

Each outcome is compared between the two groups with a test chosen in advance in the script.

Engagement change
Post-pilot score minus pre-pilot score, in points.

Test: two-group t-test (Welch)
Flight-risk score change
Post-pilot minus pre-pilot risk score.

Test: two-group t-test (Welch)
Attrition
Whether the employee left in a simulated 3-month follow-up.

Tests: two-proportion z-test and an adjusted logistic regression

4. Decide by fixed rules

Two-sided tests at 5% significance95% confidence intervals on every estimateSmallest attrition drop reliably detected: about 7.5 percentage points (80% power)

With about 266 people per group, a smaller real drop in attrition could easily go unnoticed. That is why a non-significant attrition result is read as "too small to tell", not "no effect".

Simulated findings. These results come from a synthetic workforce and simulated outcomes; they are not real-world business outcomes. Source: R/03_ab_testing_retention_intervention.R.
